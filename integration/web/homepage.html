01 / The payment lifecycle

One budget. Several moments of payment.

When the final bill unfolds over time, the authorization can stay the same.

  1. Reserve the maximum.

    The payer signs once to place 100 units in escrow, with a fixed merchant and deadlines.

  2. Collect the first part.

    The merchant captures 30. The service receives 30; 70 remains held. No new payer signature is required for this capture.

  3. Collect another part. Release the rest.

    The merchant captures 20 more and releases the unused 50 in the same operation.

  4. The hold is closed.

    The service has received 50 in total. The payer has received the unused 50. This authorization cannot be topped up or reopened.

The merchant chooses capture amounts within the agreed ceiling. Capture402 does not verify usage or service delivery.

How is a refund different?

A release returns money still held in escrow. A refund returns money already paid to the merchant. If the merchant later refunds 15, the captured total remains 50, refunded value becomes 15, and the merchant's net receipt is 35. The hold stays closed.

A refund requires the merchant's signature and enough tokens, before the refund deadline. It does not restore the spending budget.

What if the merchant stops responding?

At or after the capture deadline, the payer can submit a reclaim transaction to recover any remaining hold without the merchant or facilitator. Funds do not move automatically when the deadline passes. Previously captured payments are unaffected.

03 / Under the surface

A payment primitive your application can build around.

Your application decides what to bill. Capture402 defines who can move the funds, how much can move, and when.

Implemented in the current v1 profile

  • One funded escrow authorization with a fixed maximum and deadlines
  • Partial and repeated capture, including atomic capture-and-release
  • Merchant release and payer-direct reclaim after the capture deadline
  • Merchant-funded refunds with a separate deadline
  • Standalone Solana program and a TypeScript reference integration

Classic SPL Token assets only. Token-2022 and wrapped SOL are outside v1. Protocol fees are zero in this profile; Solana transaction fees and account rent are separate.

Start with the integration model

Start by defining the merchant, asset, ceiling and deadlines. Connect application metering to merchant-signed operations, and preserve pending outcomes until they are resolved. The repository contains reference flows; it is not a hosted billing service or a published drop-in SDK.

Where does x402 fit?

The reference integration uses x402 v2 HTTP payment transport with a local proposed Solana Auth-Capture binding. It is not an upstream-approved SVM standard, and compatibility with arbitrary x402 clients or facilitators is not claimed.

04 / Where this could fit

A ceiling now. The final bill later.

Potential applications of the primitive. These are integration ideas, not existing partnerships.

  1. 01

    Metered API sessions

    Authorize a session budget, then collect for completed work within that ceiling.

  2. 02

    Bounded compute jobs

    Reserve a maximum before a job begins; settle in parts as the work progresses.

  3. 03

    Reservations and final bills

    Agree on a maximum upfront, then capture the final amount and release the difference.
